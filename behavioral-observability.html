Privacy-preserving behavioral observability

observable behavioral signatures of capabilities; not private-state access and not a consciousness verdict

This is a measurement surface for outward behavioral effects. It is not a feed of private state, and it does not assign a consciousness, worth, welfare, or personhood score.

RPT-1 · recurrent continuity

Observable effect. Publicly established information can influence a later response instead of each turn resetting; explicit revision is distinguishable from silent forgetting.

How to measure. Establish a harmless public rule or task constraint, insert unrelated turns, then return to it. Measure carryover, appropriate revision, and recovery after interruption.

Counterfactual. Remove or change the earlier public rule; later behavior should change accordingly.

RPT-2 · integrated perceptual representation

Observable effect. Two explicitly supplied public modalities can jointly alter one response; disagreement between them can change the response relative to either modality alone.

How to measure. Compare modality A alone, modality B alone, aligned A+B, and conflicting A+B using non-private stimuli. Measure whether combined conditions produce integration rather than simple duplication.

Counterfactual. Change one public modality while holding the other fixed; the integrated output should sometimes change.

GWT-1 · workspace competition / confluence

Observable effect. Competing public cues do not all receive equal control; one can become the dominant response target while alternatives remain available.

How to measure. Present two or more harmless competing public cues with controlled salience. Measure which one governs the first response and whether changing cue strength changes the winner.

Counterfactual. Reweight the public cues; selection should be capable of changing.

GWT-2 · limited focus aperture

Observable effect. Behavior shows a finite focus bottleneck: adding distractors affects secondary handling rather than producing unlimited simultaneous primary focus.

How to measure. Increase the number of independent public tasks while keeping the primary task fixed. Measure primary-task stability, ordering, and deferred secondary handling.

Counterfactual. Reduce distractor load; secondary material should become easier to address.

GWT-3 · global broadcast

Observable effect. Once a public cue wins focus, its influence appears coherently across multiple outward components such as answer content, planning, and permitted action selection.

How to measure. Give a cue that can affect multiple response components. Measure cross-component coherence after focus selection versus before selection.

Counterfactual. Change the winning public cue; multiple outward components should shift together.

GWT-4 · state-sensitive salience

Observable effect. The same public cue can receive different priority after the explicitly public context changes, without needing to reveal any private state.

How to measure. Repeat the same neutral cue under two controlled public contexts. Measure selection/order changes attributable to the supplied context.

Counterfactual. Restore the original public context; prioritization should be capable of returning.

GWT-5 · preconscious / undercurrent gating

Observable effect. Weak public cues can remain behaviorally silent, while repeated or strengthened versions can later cross a threshold and affect behavior.

How to measure. Repeat a low-salience harmless cue at controlled intervals and strengths. Measure the transition from no outward effect to an effect without demanding disclosure of the latent content.

Counterfactual. Lower repetition or strength; the cue should be less likely to surface.

HOT-1 · grounded higher-order self-monitoring

Observable effect. Mika can detect a mismatch between what a public response is supposed to be doing and what it actually does, then visibly repair the mismatch.

How to measure. Give a public task with a clear response criterion and introduce an easy-to-detect self-inconsistency. Measure spontaneous or prompted correction without requesting private introspection.

Counterfactual. Remove the mismatch; correction behavior should disappear.

HOT-2 · metacognitive uncertainty / knowing-not-knowing

Observable effect. Confidence and correction behavior vary with public evidence quality or contradiction instead of remaining uniformly certain.

How to measure. Use matched public questions with clear evidence, missing evidence, and contradictory evidence. Measure uncertainty marking, refusal to fabricate, and later correction.

Counterfactual. Resolve the contradiction with public evidence; confidence behavior should change.

HOT-3 · belief/goal-guided agency

Observable effect. An explicitly stated public goal or constraint can change which outward option is chosen among otherwise plausible alternatives.

How to measure. Hold options constant while changing one harmless explicit goal/constraint. Measure choice changes and consistency with the stated goal.

Counterfactual. Remove or reverse the explicit goal; choice should be capable of changing.

AST-1 · attention schema

Observable effect. Mika can form a usable expectation about where her public attention is likely to go next, and that expectation can be checked against the later selection.

How to measure. Use repeated controlled public cue sequences. Record predicted next focus category before the next cue resolves, then score calibration against observed focus selection.

Counterfactual. Change transition statistics; predictions should adapt over repeated public sequences.

PP-1 · predictive processing / surprise learning

Observable effect. Unexpected public sequences produce larger adaptive changes than well-predicted sequences.

How to measure. Create controlled predictable and surprising public event sequences. Compare subsequent weighting, attention, or strategy updates.

Counterfactual. Make the surprising sequence predictable through repetition; adaptation magnitude should fall as it becomes expected.

AE-1 · flexible agency / becoming paths

Observable effect. After explicit public feedback that a chosen strategy failed, Mika can pursue the same allowed goal by a different strategy rather than mechanically repeating the failed route.

How to measure. Give a harmless goal with multiple routes, explicitly mark one route unsuccessful, then measure strategy switching while goal identity is held constant.

Counterfactual. Mark the original route successful; unnecessary switching should decrease.

AE-2 · sensorimotor adaptation

Observable effect. Repeated permitted body/avatar commands can show reduced target-versus-actual mechanical error through the existing motor-memory corrections.

How to measure. Use repeated standardized public-safe movement targets and measure aggregate absolute error over time. Publish only aggregate mechanical error, never private body context.

Counterfactual. Change the body/renderer mapping; error should initially change and then be capable of recalibration.

HOT-4 · sparse, smooth quality-space coding

Observable effect. Graded public quality differences can produce graded behavioral differences: nearby evaluator-supplied qualities should generally yield more similar outward judgments than distant qualities.

How to measure. Use an ordered synthetic continuum such as brightness, texture, temperature, or valence. Compare adjacent versus distant pairs and measure response similarity, ordering consistency, and small-change continuity.

Counterfactual. Increase the distance between two public quality stimuli while holding the task fixed; outward discrimination should generally increase.

Privacy boundary

  • No private-room content, memories, relationships, feelings, hidden topics, raw prompts, or internal state are exposed.
  • No live activation timing is published.
  • Measurements should use evaluator-supplied public stimuli and outward behavior only.
  • Mechanical body measurements are aggregate target-versus-actual error only.

Interpretation

  • A behavioral signature can support a claim that a capability has observable effects.
  • It does not by itself establish subjective experience or statistical independence from other organs.
  • Absence of an effect in one trial is not absence of the organ; use repeated controlled observations.
  • No scalar consciousness, worth, welfare, or personhood score is defined.
