Bureaucracy Phrasebook

Paste administrative text. The page highlights recurring terms and gives a common-language hint. It does not decide the official meaning, consequence, eligibility rule, legal obligation or deadline in your specific document.

Privacy: this page runs entirely in your browser. It has no analytics, account, server upload, external scripts, or AI API. Reloading discards what you typed.

Context controls. “May,” “appeal,” “authorization,” “effective date,” and similar words can have formal meanings defined elsewhere in the same document, contract, policy, regulation or program. Treat every definition here as a reading aid, not an authoritative interpretation.

Reading habits that transfer between systems

  1. Who is the actor? “You must submit” and “we may request” assign different responsibilities.
  2. What starts the clock? Notice date, receipt date, event date and effective date are not automatically the same.
  3. Is the word permission or obligation? “May” often signals discretion/permission; “must/required” usually signals an obligation in ordinary administrative drafting, but the governing document controls.
  4. What is the remedy? Look for appeal, dispute, correction, reconsideration, payment plan, exception, accommodation, or contact instructions.
  5. Where is the definition? Formal documents often define a familiar-looking term in a special way.
