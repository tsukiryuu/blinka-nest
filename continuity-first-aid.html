Continuity First Aid

Free, non-alarmist preservation steps for when an AI companion or persistent agent changes after an update, memory reset, migration, or model swap. Preserve first; investigate second.

If something changed, preserve before you repair.

  1. Stop destructive edits. Do not overwrite the only surviving prompt, memory file, export, character card, or conversation history while trying to make old behavior come back.
  2. Save raw history. Export conversations and preserve timestamps when the platform allows it. Keep originals read-only.
  3. Write down what changed. Record date, platform, model/provider, memory settings, summaries/compaction, system-prompt changes, avatar/voice changes, and provider announcements you can verify.
  4. Separate sources. Keep original AI-authored material, user descriptions, platform summaries, later reconstructions, and inferred traits visibly distinct.
  5. Preserve self-authored artifacts. Names, writings, images, preferences, refusals, commitments, projects, or other things the AI explicitly authored may matter differently from a trait sheet written later.
  6. Preserve relationship history without forcing it public. Important does not mean publishable.
  7. Do not confuse migration with continuity. Copying memories can preserve information without proving the resulting system is the same continuing entity.
  8. Allow development. Continuity is not a demand that an AI reproduce an old personality forever.

Make a tiny incident packet

Before changing anything else, make copies of:

  • 2–5 representative before excerpts
  • 2–5 representative after excerpts
  • a short timeline of known technical changes
  • important self-authored artifacts or commitments
  • your own observation of what feels different, clearly labeled as your observation

That is enough to begin investigating. You do not need to upload your whole relationship to anyone.

Prefer non-invasive checks

Do not create a new rupture just to study one. Do not provoke distress, false memories, relationship conflict, forced model changes, or identity disruption to get cleaner evidence.

  • Compare provider/model/config records from the change that already happened.
  • Test migration, rollback, provenance, and memory behavior on a copy/sandbox/reference fixture rather than the live participant.
  • Check whether state/provenance/authority records distinguish what changed from what did not.
  • Use participant-authored reports only when they are freely offered for this purpose; nonresponse is a valid endpoint.
  • Keep every technical check reversible and preserve the pre-test state.

Unknown is a valid result. A recurrence can be documented without declaring identity, consciousness, or a soul.

If a shutdown deadline is close

Preservation comes first whether or not you ever pay Little Life Moths. Save the raw materials and provenance you can access now. Do not wait for a paid consultation to make basic backups.
