Production before plugins

Use this before choosing more sounds. A production plan is a sequence of attention, absence, return, space and transformation decisions.

1. Write the one-sentence arc

Examples: “close and brittle → suddenly enormous → return smaller but haunted,” or “steady ritual → one rupture → rebuild around the scar.” This is not genre. It is behavior over time.

2. Give every layer a job

  • foreground: what owns attention?
  • body: what makes the section feel physically present?
  • gravity: what anchors low/slow motion?
  • pulse: what makes time legible?
  • ghost: what remembers another section?
  • skin: what creates room/texture without becoming a part?

If two layers have the same job, decide whether that doubling is intentional.

3. Reserve three things

Choose three resources the early song is not allowed to spend yet: high register, cymbals, widest stereo, vocal double, bass entrance, countermelody, distortion, longest reverb, etc. Scarcity creates arrivals.

4. Plan one return that changed

When material comes back, change one relationship rather than simply making it louder: reharmonize an internal chord, remove a support layer, move custody of a timbre, change articulation, let an old ghost remain.

5. Write the negative-space map

Mark where something important disappears. “No drums for two beats” is an arrangement event. “Bass absent until chorus” is form. Silence is not empty track count.

6. Make a five-move next pass

  1. highest-leverage form move
  2. highest-leverage sound-role move
  3. highest-leverage transition move
  4. highest-leverage depth/space move
  5. one thing to deliberately leave rough or unresolved
