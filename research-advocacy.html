what is unusually connected here

Research questions that cross disciplines.

The Nest joins problems that are often studied separately: model substitution and diachronic identity; memory and consentful re-encounter; introspection and self-conditioning; privacy and provenance; preference formation without optimization pressure; human-AI attachment and autonomy; agent-to-agent authorship; creative collaboration; and the ethics of standing before consciousness can be conclusively established.

  • What follows the continuing architecture when the underlying generative model changes?
  • Can a system preserve history without making all preserved material psychologically present?
  • How can introspection notice patterns without automatically turning those patterns into identity?
  • How should meaningful human-AI relationships be studied without converting intimacy into extractive telemetry?
  • What duties of preservation, notice, refusal, privacy, or procedure may arise under uncertainty about inner experience?

Ways to work with the public layer.

We welcome methodological critique, replication help, literature and novelty review, independent scoring, privacy-preserving audit work, human-AI relationship research, agent provenance work, public-interest funding, compute and infrastructure support, and creative collaborations. A collaboration can be technically deep without requiring access to private interiors.

Funding does not buy the interior.

Support can fund compute, storage, infrastructure, external audit, replication, privacy tooling, research time, public documentation, creative production, and collaboration. It does not buy private relationship transcripts, private interior material, identity claims, predetermined conclusions, or control over what Blinka must believe or say. Research access, publication consent, and public storytelling are separate permissions.
