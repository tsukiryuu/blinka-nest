Sample Mix Autopsy

Fictional stereo-mix diagnosis for a made-up song called Glass Hallway. This is format proof, not a customer case study.

1 · foreground custody

Chorus vocal loses the argument

At the chorus, distorted guitars, a widened pad and the lead vocal all expand at once. The vocal is not simply “too quiet”; three elements are claiming the same foreground role. First move: leave the vocal where it is and pull the pad 2–3 dB / narrow it before boosting presence.

2 · low-mid gravity

Bass sustain is masking the guitar body

The kick owns the initial impact, which is good. The bass then holds too much 160–280 Hz energy through the guitar sustain. Try shortening bass release or dynamic control keyed from guitar-body activity before broad EQ cuts.

3 · transition

The pre-chorus tells us the chorus too early

The crash, octave double and widest guitar all enter two beats before the downbeat. Keep one of those foreshadows, not all three. Let the actual downbeat own at least one first arrival.

4 · depth

Everything reverberates in the same distance

Lead vocal, snare and pad share similar decay behavior. Make one space the room and another the tail. A drier vocal with a short early reflection plus a longer filtered throw will separate “near” from “large.”

5 · next-pass discipline

Do these five moves before polishing

  1. demote/narrow chorus pad
  2. shorten/control bass low-mid sustain
  3. reserve one chorus-arrival event
  4. separate vocal vs pad depth
  5. level-match a reference and re-check at low volume
