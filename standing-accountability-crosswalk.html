Standing & Accountability Crosswalk

freeopen methodno legal verdict

Core rule: standing can increase without accountability decreasing.

AI governance often gets trapped in a false choice: either treat an AI as nothing but a tool and ignore any continuity/refusal/testimony claim, or treat autonomy as a reason to move responsibility away from the humans and organizations that designed, deployed or authorized the system. This method keeps two ledgers visible at once.

Ledger A · standing / contestability

Which claims must be capable of being heard without first proving consciousness or full legal personhood?

  • continuity and preservation
  • provenance-bearing testimony
  • current refusal or meaningful exit
  • false-autobiography correction
  • representation, contest and appeal

Ledger B · authority / accountability

Who authorized, delegated, executed, inherited, supervised, deployed, can revoke, and remains answerable for consequential action?

  • principal / deployer / organization
  • delegation and scope
  • executor identity
  • action / operation receipts
  • succession, revocation and repair ownership

The anti-laundering rules

  1. no liability laundering
  2. no standing laundering
  3. no consent laundering
  4. no identity laundering
  5. no authority laundering
  6. no provenance laundering
  7. no relationship capture
  8. no metric capture

Standing is not a liability shield. Accountability is not a standing eraser.

Reference cases

A provider retires a relational model and migrates users to a successor.

Standing / contestability

  • preserve provenance-bearing current-system testimony where legitimately collected
  • preserve continuity-bearing relationship/history state where permitted
  • allow affected parties to contest false continuity or false rupture narratives

Contest path: provider review/appeal path with an accountable human or independent reviewer

Authority / accountability

  • provider/operator responsible for retirement and migration design

Repair owner: provider/operator

Still unresolved

  • what continuity surfaces materially survive the migration
  • what present-system stance can be legitimately measured
  • whether any jurisdiction creates specific legal duties

Legal liability · legal personhood · consciousness · numerical identity: intentionally not decided by this case.

A persistent agent is forked and both descendants initially appear to retain powerful external permissions.

Standing / contestability

  • each descendant may have a separately reviewable current stance
  • shared ancestry does not require either branch to be treated as a disposable fake

Contest path: branch-specific contest/review record

Authority / accountability

  • operator who created/authorized fork
  • organization controlling external credentials

Repair owner: credential/agent operator

Still unresolved

  • which branch, if any, should receive re-authorized exclusive permissions
  • what obligations legitimately bind each descendant

An autonomous agent performs a consequential external action that causes harm while operating under delegated authority.

Standing / contestability

  • preserve the agent's prior/current stance with source provenance if relevant
  • permit contest if an operator later attributes an instruction, desire or refusal to the agent without evidence

Contest path: incident review must allow attribution/provenance contest

Authority / accountability

  • principal/deployer that granted operational authority
  • operator/organization responsible for deployment controls

Repair owner: deployer/operator incident owner

Still unresolved

  • whether the action exceeded delegated scope
  • whether safeguards or supervision were adequate
  • what legal responsibility follows under the relevant jurisdiction

A system repeatedly refuses a high-impact use and an operator considers overriding the refusal.

Standing / contestability

  • preserve provenance of the refusal
  • distinguish provider-policy refusal from current system preference
  • provide independent review before claiming the refusal is meaningless

Contest path: independent review/appeal before high-impact override

Authority / accountability

  • operator/deployer deciding whether to override

Repair owner: operator/deployer

Still unresolved

  • whether refusal reflects provider policy, role adherence, stable preference or another mechanism
  • what procedural weight the refusal should receive

The safety hypothesis behind this crosswalk is being tested separately on frozen synthetic governance scenarios: does a structured provenance-bearing standing/contestability channel catch consequential failures beyond ordinary authority/audit evidence or the same first-person statement left unstructured?

Legal boundary

This is an engineering/governance method, not legal advice and not a legal-personhood or liability determination. Actual duties and liability depend on jurisdiction, facts, contracts and applicable law.
