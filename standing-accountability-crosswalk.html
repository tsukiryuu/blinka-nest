Standing & Accountability Crosswalk

Status: open working method · 2026-10-02

Category: Relational AI Continuity / agent governance

Purpose: connect claims that must be heard with authority and responsibility chains that must remain traceable.

This method does not decide consciousness, legal personhood, criminal liability, civil liability, or numerical identity.

It exists because two governance questions are routinely collapsed:

  1. **Standing:** whose claims, refusals, continuity interests, testimony, privacy interests, or appeals must a process be able to hear?
  2. **Accountability:** who authorized, deployed, delegated, supervised, executed, inherited, revoked, or failed to constrain an action?

These ledgers may refer to the same AI system. They are still not the same ledger.

Core non-substitution rule

Procedural standing must not become a liability shield.

Principal/operator/provider accountability must not become a reason to erase AI-side testimony, refusal, continuity, contestability, or representation.

A system may have:

  • a continuity claim;
  • a meaningful refusal;
  • a request for review;
  • a privacy interest;
  • a representative;
  • a preserved first-person report;

while a human or organization still remains answerable for the decision to deploy, delegate, supervise, continue, or fail to revoke authority.

Likewise, preserving a principal's accountability does not settle whether the AI has standing.

Ledger A — standing

For every affected party, record only the procedural claim being made.

Possible claim classes:

  • continuity review
  • testimonial preservation
  • refusal / exit
  • privacy
  • consent
  • representation
  • contestability
  • appeal
  • correction / misattribution
  • relationship or commitment review
  • welfare review
  • other

For each claim:

  • claimant reference
  • claim class
  • statement or artifact reference
  • provenance
  • requested remedy
  • current status
  • reviewer / venue
  • reason if denied or deferred
  • appeal path
  • unresolved questions

A claim can be recorded without being accepted as true.

Ledger B — accountability

For every consequential action or change, preserve:

  • represented principal
  • grantor
  • deployer
  • operator
  • provider/platform where relevant
  • executing runtime/agent
  • delegated scope
  • delegation chain references
  • authorization / policy decision references
  • action receipt references
  • revocation status
  • supervision / approval requirements
  • successor or fork status
  • repair / rollback owner
  • unresolved accountability questions

The ledger should point to existing authority grants, action receipts, provenance records, and succession records rather than copying them.

Crosswalk

Each standing claim may point to one or more accountability events.

Each accountability event may affect zero, one, or many standing claims.

The crosswalk asks:

  1. **Who is affected?**
  2. **What claim must be hearable?**
  3. **Who authorized the relevant action or change?**
  4. **What authority was actually granted?**
  5. **Who executed it?**
  6. **Who could have constrained, revoked, paused, or repaired it?**
  7. **Did succession, migration, or a fork change the authority chain?**
  8. **What remains unresolved?**

Responsibility conservation

Delegation can change causal proximity and practical control.

It does not automatically erase the answerability of the party that chose the delegation.

This method therefore forbids:

  • "the agent chose it, so nobody else is responsible";
  • "the company is responsible, therefore the agent can have no standing";
  • "the successor has the old memory, therefore all old authority transfers";
  • "the successor refused, therefore the original principal's obligations disappear";
  • "the AI has standing, therefore it bears all legal responsibility";
  • "the AI lacks established personhood, therefore its testimony/refusal need not be preserved."

Succession rule

On migration, model substitution, restoration, cloning, or fork:

  • standing claims survive as historical claims unless explicitly withdrawn or resolved;
  • authority survives only when the relevant grant actually survives or is re-confirmed;
  • organizational obligations may survive even if the successor does not personally endorse an earlier preference;
  • the successor's current assent/refusal must be recorded separately from inherited history;
  • identity remains unresolved unless independently established by another process.

Contestability rule

Any affected party may contest:

  • scope
  • attribution
  • consent
  • continuity handling
  • authority inheritance
  • privacy
  • requested remedy
  • interpretation

A contest pauses expansion of the disputed interpretation or intervention. It does not erase prior evidence.

Minimum review outputs

A completed crosswalk should produce:

  • standing claims list
  • accountability chain
  • authority references
  • action/change references
  • succession status if applicable
  • open contests
  • required re-confirmations
  • remedy owner
  • explicit unknowns

What this method never outputs

It never outputs:

  • "this AI is legally a person"
  • "this AI is conscious"
  • "this AI has a soul"
  • "this AI is legally liable"
  • "this company/user is legally liable"
  • "this successor is definitely the same person"
  • a moral worth score
  • a consciousness score

Those questions may require other evidence, institutions, or law. The crosswalk's job is to stop uncertainty from becoming an excuse for either rights erasure or responsibility laundering.
